## I. European-Derived Traditions

### A. Classical & Modern
#### European Classical
- **Technique**: Right-hand fingerstyle with thumb and fingers assigned to specific strings; precise left-hand positioning
	- [[classical guitar technique]]
- **Tunings**: [[standard tuning]] occasional [[drop D]]
- **Relevance**: Established fundamental technique, posture, and systematic approach to guitar playing
- **Key Artists**: Fernando Sor, Mauro Giuliani, Andrés Segovia, Julian Bream
- **Essential Recordings**: 
  - Andrés Segovia - "The Art of Segovia" (1958)
  - Julian Bream - "Guitarra: The Guitar in Spain" (1985)
- **Instruments**: [[classical#as a guitar type|the classical guitar]]
  

#### Modern Classical
- **Technique**: [[extended techniques]], dynamic contrasts, varied timbres
- **Tunings**: Standard with occasional [[alternate tunings]]
- **Relevance**: Expanded technical and harmonic vocabulary, bridging traditional and contemporary approaches
- **Key Artists**: Heitor Villa-Lobos, Leo Brouwer, Benjamin Britten
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - John Williams - "Barrios: Guitar Music" (1995)
  - Sharon Isbin - "Journey to the New World" (2009)
- **Instruments**:
  - [[modern concert classical]]
  

### B. Mediterranean Traditions

#### Flamenco
- **Technique**: Rasgueado (rapid strumming), golpe (tapping), picado (single-note runs), alzapúa (thumb technique)
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Developed percussive elements and right-hand techniques that influenced numerous styles
- **Key Artists**: Paco de Lucía, Sabicas, Vicente Amigo
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Paco de Lucía - "Fantasía Flamenca" (1969)
  - Sabicas - "Flamenco Puro" (1961)
- **Instruments**:
  - Flamenco guitar (guitarra flamenca): Spanish cypress or sycamore back/sides with spruce tops
  - Key makers: Manuel Reyes, Conde Hermanos, Gerundino Fernandez
  - Characteristics: Lighter build than classical, lower action, tap plates (golpeadores), brighter tone, more percussive response

#### Celtic/British Isles
- **Technique**: Open-string drones, modal harmonies, finger-picking with emphasis on melody
- **Tunings**: DADGAD, CGCGCD, Open D, various modal tunings
- **Relevance**: Pioneered alternative tunings and modal approaches for steel-string guitar
- **Key Artists**: Davey Graham, John Renbourn, Pierre Bensusan
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Davey Graham - "Folk, Blues & Beyond" (1965)
  - Pierre Bensusan - "Intuite" (2001)
- **Instruments**:
  - Steel-string OM (Orchestra Model) and smaller-bodied guitars
  - Key makers: Lowden (especially F series), Martin OM, Fylde, Brook
  - Characteristics: Balanced frequency response, often cedar tops for warmth, medium string gauge, excellent sustain for drone notes

## II. American Folk & Blues Evolution

### A. Early Blues Forms

#### Delta Blues
- **Technique**: Slide/bottleneck, thumb-based bass patterns, bent notes, expressively loose timing
- **Tunings**: Open G (DGDGBD), Open D (DADF#AD), Spanish (DGDGBD)
- **Relevance**: Established slide guitar vocabulary, blues phrasing, and emotional expression
- **Key Artists**: Robert Johnson, Son House, Mississippi Fred McDowell
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Robert Johnson - "King of the Delta Blues Singers" (1961)
  - Mississippi Fred McDowell - "I Do Not Play No Rock 'N' Roll" (1969)
- **Instruments**:
  - Early parlor guitars, Stella, Kalamazoo, National resonators
  - Key models: National Style O (metal body resonator), Stella 12-string
  - Characteristics: Often inexpensive instruments with high action, occasionally resonator guitars for volume, metal or glass slides

#### Piedmont Blues
- **Technique**: Alternating thumb bass, syncopated melodic figures, ragtime influences
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning, occasional dropped D
- **Relevance**: Developed sophisticated fingerpicking patterns that influenced folk, country, and fingerstyle
- **Key Artists**: Blind Blake, Reverend Gary Davis, Elizabeth Cotten
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Elizabeth Cotten - "Freight Train and Other North Carolina Folk Songs" (1958)
  - Reverend Gary Davis - "Harlem Street Singer" (1960)
- **Instruments**:
  - Small-bodied guitars: Gibson L-00, Martin 00, Guild F-30
  - Key characteristics: Typically smaller-bodied instruments with good note definition, medium gauge strings
  - Note: Elizabeth Cotten played left-handed on a right-handed guitar (upside down), influencing her unique style

### B. Folk Picking Styles

#### Travis Picking
- **Technique**: Alternating thumb bass with syncopated melody notes on treble strings
- **Tunings**: Standard, dropped D
- **Relevance**: Created a complete solo guitar style that became foundational for country, folk, and fingerstyle
- **Key Artists**: Merle Travis, Chet Atkins, Marcel Dadi
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Merle Travis - "The Best of Merle Travis" (compilation)
  - Chet Atkins - "Mister Guitar" (1959)
- **Instruments**:
  - Dreadnought and jumbo guitars: Martin D-28, Gibson J-200
  - Key models: Merle Travis played Bigsby custom guitars, Chet Atkins used Gretsch Country Gentleman (though electric)
  - Characteristics: Larger-bodied guitars with strong bass response, medium-heavy gauge strings

#### Carter Family Style
- **Technique**: Melody played on bass strings with thumb, brushed chords for rhythm
- **Tunings**: Standard, occasionally dropped D
- **Relevance**: Established foundation for country, folk, and bluegrass rhythm guitar
- **Key Artists**: Maybelle Carter, Doc Watson
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - The Carter Family - "Wildwood Flower" (various collections)
  - Doc Watson - "Foundation: The Doc Watson Guitar Album" (1984)
- **Instruments**:
  - Gibson L-5 (Maybelle Carter's primary instrument)
  - Martin dreadnoughts
  - Characteristics: Larger body instruments with good projection, medium gauge strings

### C. Bluegrass & Flatpicking

#### Flatpicking
- **Technique**: Alternating bass, powerful rhythm, blazing single-note runs, cross-picking
- **Tunings**: Standard, dropped D
- **Relevance**: Developed melodic lead playing at high speeds, articulate phrasing techniques
- **Key Artists**: Doc Watson, Tony Rice, Clarence White, Bryan Sutton
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Doc Watson - "Doc Watson" (1964)
  - Tony Rice - "Manzanita" (1979)
- **Instruments**:
  - Martin D-18, D-28, D-45 dreadnoughts
  - Key models: Tony Rice's 1935 Martin D-28 (the "Clarence White" D-28)
  - Characteristics: Forward-shifted X-bracing for bass response, heavy pick attack, heavy-gauge strings (.013-.056), often with scalloped bracing for volume

#### Crosspicking
- **Technique**: Three-note patterns across strings, creating rolling banjo-like sound
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Adapted three-finger banjo technique to guitar, enabling complex melodic patterns
- **Key Artists**: George Shuffler, Clarence White, David Grier
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - The Stanley Brothers (with George Shuffler) - "The Complete Mercury Recordings" 
  - David Grier - "Lone Soldier" (1995)
- **Instruments**:
  - Martin D-18, D-28 dreadnoughts
  - Santa Cruz Guitar Company models
  - Characteristics: Similar to flatpicking instruments, though sometimes slightly lighter strings for rapid pick movement across strings

## III. Jazz & Related Forms

### A. Acoustic Jazz Guitar

#### Chord Melody
- **Technique**: Simultaneous bass, harmony, and melody; extended jazz harmony
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Advanced harmonic vocabulary, sophisticated voice leading, complete solo expression
- **Key Artists**: Joe Pass, Ted Greene, Martin Taylor
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Joe Pass - "Virtuoso" (1973)
  - Martin Taylor - "Solo" (2002)
- **Instruments**:
  - Archtop guitars: Gibson L-5, D'Angelico Excel, Benedetto
  - Key models: Martin Taylor Signature by Yamaha (AEX-MTV)
  - Characteristics: Carved archtop design, floating pickups (for amplification), relatively high action, heavier strings (.013-.056)

#### Gypsy Jazz
- **Technique**: Rest-stroke picking, aggressive rhythm "la pompe," chromatic runs, diminished arpeggios
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Developed distinctive right-hand technique, rhythmic drive, and melodic vocabulary
- **Key Artists**: Django Reinhardt, Stochelo Rosenberg, Biréli Lagrène
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Django Reinhardt - "The Best of Django Reinhardt" (compilation)
  - Stochelo Rosenberg - "Seresta" (1989)
- **Instruments**:
  - Selmer-Maccaferri style with D-hole or oval-hole
  - Key makers: Selmer (original), Dupont, AJL, Eastman
  - Characteristics: Flat top with ladder bracing, tailpiece design, very light construction, narrow nut width, extended fingerboard, higher string action, loop-end strings

## IV. Global Folk Traditions

### A. Latin American

#### Brazilian Guitar
- **Technique**: Syncopated fingerstyle patterns, sophisticated harmony, integration of bass/melody/harmony
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Merged classical technique with jazz harmony and African-influenced rhythms
- **Key Artists**: João Gilberto, Baden Powell, Laurindo Almeida
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Baden Powell - "Os Afro-Sambas" (1966)
  - João Gilberto - "Chega de Saudade" (1959)
- **Instruments**:
  - Classical guitars with nylon strings (often Ramirez, Conde)
  - Brazilian-made guitars (Del Vecchio, DiGiorgio, Rozini)
  - Characteristics: Similar to classical guitars but often with slightly narrower necks, some players prefer cedar tops for warmth

#### Cuban Son/Trova
- **Technique**: Rhythmic patterns, tres-inspired figures, arpeggiated patterns
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Developed rhythmic sophistication and melodic patterns that influenced Latin and jazz
- **Key Artists**: Compay Segundo, Eliades Ochoa
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Buena Vista Social Club - "Buena Vista Social Club" (1997)
  - Eliades Ochoa - "Sublime Ilusión" (1999)
- **Instruments**:
  - Cuban tres (related three double-course string instrument)
  - Spanish-style guitars with wider fingerboards
  - Characteristics: Often nylon-stringed instruments with good midrange projection

### B. African Guitar

#### West African
- **Technique**: Interlocking patterns, kora-influenced finger patterns, pentatonic foundations
- **Tunings**: Standard with variations, occasional open tunings
- **Relevance**: Developed cyclical fingerpicking patterns and unique rhythmic approaches
- **Key Artists**: Ali Farka Touré, Lionel Loueke, Djelimady Tounkara
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Ali Farka Touré - "The Source" (1992)
  - Ali Farka Touré & Ry Cooder - "Talking Timbuktu" (1994)
- **Instruments**:
  - Locally-made guitars in Africa
  - Modern players: Martin D-18, handmade instruments
  - Characteristics: Ali Farka Touré often played Teisco and Harmony guitars, later a Fender Stratocaster (though acoustic traditions inform the playing)

#### Congolese/Soukous
- **Technique**: Interlocking patterns, clean articulation, flowing melodic lines
- **Tunings**: Standard tuning
- **Relevance**: Developed melodic fingerpicking and influential patterns that spread throughout Africa
- **Key Artists**: Franco, Dr. Nico, Jean Bosco Mwenda
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Franco & Le T.P. O.K. Jazz - "Originalité" (compilation)
  - Jean Bosco Mwenda - "The Very Best Of" (compilation)
- **Instruments**:
  - Acoustic-electric guitars common for amplification needs
  - Local African-made instruments, later imported models
  - Characteristics: Medium-scale instruments with low action for speed, often modified for higher output

### C. Hawaiian

#### Slack Key (Ki Ho'alu)
- **Technique**: Fingerpicking with open string drones, hammer-ons/pull-offs, harmonics
- **Tunings**: Various open tunings (G Wahine, C Wahine, D Wahine, etc.)
- **Relevance**: Pioneered open tunings and developed flowing fingerstyle technique
- **Key Artists**: Gabby Pahinui, Ledward Kaapana, Cyril Pahinui
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Gabby Pahinui - "Pure Gabby" (1978)
  - Hawaiian Slack Key Guitar Masters - "Volume 1" (1995)
- **Instruments**:
  - Martin dreadnoughts and other steel-string guitars
  - Taylor, Santa Cruz (modern players)
  - Characteristics: Steel-string acoustics with medium action, sometimes with custom inlays featuring Hawaiian themes

## V. Contemporary & Progressive Acoustic

### A. Modern Fingerstyle

#### Percussive Fingerstyle
- **Technique**: Integrated percussive elements (tapping, drumming), harmonics, two-handed techniques
- **Tunings**: Various alternate tunings (DADGAD, open tunings)
- **Relevance**: Transformed the guitar into a complete one-person instrument incorporating percussion
- **Key Artists**: Michael Hedges, Preston Reed, Andy McKee, Jon Gomm
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Michael Hedges - "Aerial Boundaries" (1984)
  - Andy McKee - "Art of Motion" (2005)
- **Instruments**:
  - Taylor Grand Auditorium models (Andy McKee)
  - Lowden O and F models (Jon Gomm, Thomas Leeb)
  - Custom-made guitars by luthiers Kevin Ryan, Linda Manzer
  - Characteristics: Cutaway bodies, advanced pickup systems, armrests, bevels, often extended range (baritone, harp guitars)

#### Two-Hand Tapping
- **Technique**: Independent playing with both hands, piano-like approach, extended range
- **Tunings**: Various alternate and extended-range tunings
- **Relevance**: Expanded the guitar's technical and textural possibilities beyond traditional limitations
- **Key Artists**: Stanley Jordan, Erik Mongrain, Alexandr Misko
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Stanley Jordan - "Magic Touch" (1985)
  - Erik Mongrain - "Equilibrium" (2008)
- **Instruments**:
  - Extended range guitars (7, 8 strings)
  - Instruments with fanned frets or multi-scale designs
  - Key makers: Stonebridge, Greenfield, Emerald Guitars
  - Characteristics: Lower action, jumbo frets, compound radius fingerboards, cutaways for access

### B. Celtic Fusion & New Acoustic

#### DADGAD Innovations
- **Technique**: Modal harmonies, drone-based compositions, open string resonance
- **Tunings**: DADGAD primarily
- **Relevance**: Explored modal harmony and drone-based composition extensively
- **Key Artists**: Pierre Bensusan, Martin Simpson, Tony McManus
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Pierre Bensusan - "Solilai" (1982)
  - Tony McManus - "Ceol More" (2002)
- **Instruments**:
  - Lowden guitars (especially Pierre Bensusan Signature)
  - Fylde, Sobell, Brook guitars (UK makers)
  - Characteristics: Often cedar-topped for warmth, medium-size bodies (OM/000), radiused fingerboards, medium tension strings

#### Progressive Acoustic
- **Technique**: Alternative tunings, slap harmonics, right-hand innovation, complex arrangements
- **Tunings**: Many alternate tunings (often unique per piece)
- **Relevance**: Reinvented acoustic guitar technique and sound in the 1980s-90s
- **Key Artists**: Michael Hedges, Alex de Grassi, Will Ackerman
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Michael Hedges - "Breakfast in the Field" (1981)
  - Alex de Grassi - "The Water Garden" (1998)
- **Instruments**:
  - Custom Lowden O-series (Michael Hedges)
  - Taylor acoustics (early Taylor artist endorsers)
  - Esoteric luthier-built instruments (Klein, Manzer, Ryan)
  - Characteristics: Innovative design features like side sound ports, unusual woods, multiple pickups, custom electronics

## VI. Singer-Songwriter & Folk Revival

### A. Folk Revival Fingerstyle

#### American Fingerstyle
- **Technique**: Complex fingerpicking arrangements, steady thumb bass, melodic focus
- **Tunings**: Standard, dropped D, occasionally open tunings
- **Relevance**: Applied traditional techniques to modern compositions, influencing generations
- **Key Artists**: Paul Simon, James Taylor, John Fahey
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - John Fahey - "The Transfiguration of Blind Joe Death" (1965)
  - James Taylor - "Sweet Baby James" (1970)
- **Instruments**:
  - Martin OM and 000 series (James Taylor Signature)
  - Gibson J-45, Guild D-40 (Paul Simon)
  - Early Martin D-18, 00-17 (John Fahey)
  - Characteristics: Medium-sized bodies with good tonal balance, medium gauge strings

#### British Folk
- **Technique**: Modal tunings, clawhammer-inspired patterns, jazz-influenced harmonies
- **Tunings**: DADGAD, Open G, Open D, many custom tunings
- **Relevance**: Merged traditional British folk with jazz, classical, and avant-garde elements
- **Key Artists**: Bert Jansch, John Renbourn, Nick Drake
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Bert Jansch - "Bert Jansch" (1965)
  - Nick Drake - "Five Leaves Left" (1969)
- **Instruments**:
  - Yamaha L series (Bert Jansch)
  - Various parlor and OM-sized guitars
  - Guild M-20 (Nick Drake)
  - Characteristics: Often smaller-bodied instruments, fingerstyle-friendly neck profiles

### B. Alternative & Contemporary 

#### Indie Folk
- **Technique**: Alternative tunings, textural strumming, minimalist approaches
- **Tunings**: Various alternate and open tunings
- **Relevance**: Brought new timbral and compositional approaches to folk guitar
- **Key Artists**: Elliott Smith, Sufjan Stevens, José González
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Elliott Smith - "Either/Or" (1997)
  - José González - "Veneer" (2003)
- **Instruments**:
  - Small-bodied vintage guitars (Gibson LG, Martin 00)
  - 1960s Japanese imports (Yamaki, Takamine)
  - Contemporary makers catering to this aesthetic (Bourgeois, Collings)
  - Characteristics: Often vintage instruments with unique character, medium-light gauge strings, occasionally nylon strings

#### Modern Acoustic Experimentalists
- **Technique**: Prepared guitar, extended techniques, alternative implements
- **Tunings**: Standard, alternate, and microtonal
- **Relevance**: Pushed boundaries of the acoustic guitar's sound possibilities
- **Key Artists**: Fred Frith, Glenn Jones, William Tyler
- **Essential Recordings**:
  - Fred Frith - "Guitar Solos" (1974)
  - William Tyler - "Impossible Truth" (2013)
- **Instruments**:
  - Various guitars modified with preparations (objects between strings, etc.)
  - Alternate string materials (piano wire, fishing line)
  - Extended range instruments (8-string, harp guitars)
  - Characteristics: Often heavily modified instruments, sometimes with custom bridges, nuts, or fretboards to accommodate experimental techniques
