> [!tldr] tldr
> Music where the concept of [[modes]] is used to organise the [[harmony]] in opposition to [[tonal music]] which uses [[tonal music|functional harmony]]. 

Modal music refers to compositions where the underlying organisation doesn't use the concepts of [[tonal music|functional harmony]] but rather [[modes]]. This means the [[sub dominant]], [[dominant]] and [[tonic]] are not necessarily of their usual nature. [[tension and release]] is expressed through other means than the traditional [[V - I|perfect cadence]]. The music will be constructed around the particular sound of a mode, for example the Major sound with a [[Sharp 11th]] of the [[lydian]] scale. The [[melody]] and [[harmony]] will make use of the chosen mode.

This creates a more textural sound where the harmony paints a certain palette, it creates an ambiance rather than the clear narrative effect of functional harmony with its turning points and punctuation.

In order to create contrast and [[tension and release]], modal music will sometimes move from one modal colour to another, either by varying the mode used, the root but not the mode or both the root and mode.
