When first starting to learn the [[guitar]], especially on your own, there are a lot of bad habits and tendencies one can develop. There are also a lot of questions one might have, issues that when left unresolved can create real problems down the road. 

These can range from bad [[posture]], wrong [[technique]], unproductive [[practice]] habits or [[music theory]] misunderstandings.
# postural bad habits
The guitar can be an awkward object to manipulate, its design is not the friendliest to the human body. It is very easy to develop an unhealthy physical relationship to the instrument that can lead to real injuries. 

As a general rule, if something hurts – especially if it still hurts when not playing – you're doing it wrong. 

Special note for left hand fingertips, yes they will hurt. But you should keep playing, you'll grow calluses in no time.

Go through my [[posture]] advice and pay close attention to your spine, shoulders and wrists.

You want to lay a solid technical foundation as early as possible so that you don't have to think about it later down the road. It's much harder to unlearn something and replace it with a correct habit compared to learning it right the first time. Spend time making sure every movement, especially the fundamentals of [[left hand]] and [[right hand]], are learnt correctly. This will save you hours of frustration.

# unproductive practice
Just like when starting strength training, a beginner will make fast 'nooby gains' even with sub optimal practice and eventually hit a plateau. In order to maximise progress, shorten plateaux and develop a healthy relationship with practicing that is long lasting and rewarding; one should spend time honing the basics. 

I have seen countless guitar players who could do some quite advanced technical stuff while not knowing their simple [[triads]] and their [[inversions]]. This is trying to run before you can walk. Don't rush through material driven by your ego. Find a good course or teacher and follow the steps, don't move on until you have mastered the current step.

# theoretical misunderstanding
When learning [[music theory]], make sure you fully understand the basic building blocks of music. Don't get lost in online rabbit holes about modes or scales until you are well familiar with [[the 12 semitones]], [[intervals]] and [[tonality]]. Old music theory books made students go through numerous exercises before introducing more advanced notions and they did it for a reason. Try to get a solid grasp of the basic notions until you can explain them clearly to someone else and only then move on.
