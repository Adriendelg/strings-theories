# jazz as a genre
Jazz is a music genre originating in the US around the beginning of the 20th century. Drawing from [[blues]], [[ragtime]] and [[tonal music|functional harmony]] it makes use of [[improvisation]], [[swing]] and a wide variety of inspirations. 

Just like [[classical music]], jazz is now an umbrella term that can mean a lot of very different music. [[jazz history|Jazz history]] shows that there are many sub genres with very specific languages and specificities. Ranging from [[big band]] playing [[swing]], to blowing [[hard bop]] and [[progressive metal]] inspired concept albums, jazz is now more of an approach to music than it is a fixed genre. 

# jazz as a repertoire
Jazz is also a [[repertoire]] of songs, these are the most played and improvised on tunes for decades called [[jazz standards]].

# jazz as a language
Jazz is also a [[musical language]] with its vocabulary, grammar and accent. Jazz played today is the modern fusion of different movements of [[jazz history]]. 

Modern jazz players draw from:
- [[blues]]
- [[bebop]]
- [[modal music]]
- [[rock]]
- [[modern classical music]]
- many other styles

Typical jazz language includes melodic elements such as:
- a mix of [[tonal music]] and [[modal music]]
- heavy use of [[chromaticism]]
	- [[enclosures]]
	- [[approach tones]]
- [[extended harmony]]
- [[chord tone|chord tones]]
- wide variety of [[rhythm|rhythms]] and [[time feel]]
	- [[swing]]
	- [[straight time]]
	- [[compound time|odd meters]]

# jazz as an approach to music
Jazz is also a kind of musical philosophy, the emphasis on [[improvisation]] and uniqueness of every performance is a key element. Historically, jazz has often made use of the popular music of its days. Reinterpreting popular songs and using them as a vessel for improvisation. Seeing songs as an object to play with, to twist and stretch on the spot to create something new is a core element of what jazz is.
