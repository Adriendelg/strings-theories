> [!tldr] Definition
> A mode is a scale created by using the [[intervals|interval]] formula of a [[parent scales|parent scale]] and starting it on another degree than the traditional I. 

# how modes work
## scale as formula
Every [[scales|scale]] has a specific interval formula, the [[major scale]], for example is composed of this specific series of Whole and Half steps.

  ![[C Major Scale Diagram.svg]]

This series of intervals can also be represented on a circle to illustrate the cyclical nature of a scale repeating every octave.

![[Circular Major Scale Diagram (1).svg]]


##  different starting point, new scales within scales

To find the modes of the major scale we start this pattern of half and whole steps on different [[scale degrees]]. 

Starting this cycle on interval from the next note, D gives us this:

![[D Dorian Mode Circle.svg]]
The cycle of interval is still the same, only now it starts on the second note giving us this interval formula WHWWWHW. Note that the 7 pitches haven't changed, we are still playing the white keys of the piano, only the starting point of the structure has changed.

This new interval formula is called the [[dorian]] scale.

Looking at the intervals from our new [[tonic]] D we get this formula:
R M2 m3 P4 P5 M6 m7

We can repeat this process on every scale degree.

## from every scale degree

| Mode                        | Step Formula  | 1   | 2   | 3   | 4    | 5    | 6   | 7   |
| --------------------------- | ------------- | --- | --- | --- | ---- | ---- | --- | --- |
| [[Ionian]] (Major)          | W-W-H-W-W-W-H | R   | M2  | M3  | P4   | P5   | M6  | M7  |
| [[Dorian]]                  | W-H-W-W-W-H-W | R   | M2  | m3  | P4   | P5   | M6  | m7  |
| [[Phrygian]]                | H-W-W-W-H-W-W | R   | m2  | m3  | P4   | P5   | m6  | m7  |
| [[Lydian]]                  | W-W-W-H-W-W-H | R   | M2  | M3  | aug4 | P5   | M6  | M7  |
| [[Mixolydian]]              | W-W-H-W-W-H-W | R   | M2  | M3  | P4   | P5   | M6  | m7  |
| [[Aeolian]] (Natural Minor) | W-H-W-W-H-W-W | R   | M2  | m3  | P4   | P5   | m6  | m7  |
| [[Locrian]]                 | H-W-W-H-W-W-W | R   | m2  | m3  | P4   | dim5 | m6  | m7  |

These are all the modes of the major scale and their formula and interval composition.

## the common misconception
When first learning modes, it is easy to understand the concept of starting a scale on a different degree. Playing C Major from E to E for example, playing an E [[Phrygian]] scale. But isn't it just playing C Major? After all it's still just the white notes of the piano.

> [!important] How to learn the modes
> This is why modes need to be studied and compared from the same root note in order to understand their identity, their sound. We want to listen to every mode from the same [[tonic]].

Let's take every mode's formula and apply it to a C root.

| Mode                    | Pitches            | Characteristic intervals | Parent scale |
| ----------------------- | ------------------ | ------------------------ | ------------ |
| [[major scale\|ionian]] | C D E F G A B      | /                        | C            |
| [[dorian]]              | C D Eb F G A Bb    | b3 b7                    | Bb           |
| [[phrygian]]            | C Db Db F G Ab Bb  | b2 b3 b6 b7              | Ab           |
| [[lydian]]              | C D E F# G A B     | #4                       | G            |
| [[mixolydian]]          | C D E F G A Bb     | b7                       | F            |
| [[aeolian]]             | C D Eb F G Ab Bb   | b3 b6 b7                 | Eb           |
| [[locrian]]             | C Db Eb F Gb Ab Bb | b2 b3 b5 b6 b7           | Db           |
Characteristic interval here refers to the notes that deviate from the [[major scale]] or [[ionian]] mode and parent scale means the original major scale from which these modes are derived. 

## other parent scales
The same principles can be applied to any other 7 notes to the octave scales such as:
- [[melodic minor scale]]
- [[harmonic minor scale]]
- [[harmonic major scale]]
- [[double harmonic major scale]]


> [!NOTE] major scale modes as families
> When studying the modes of other parent scales, the modes of the major scale become reference points. In studying these other scales and their modes, we will encounter other dorians, phrygians, lydians etc.
